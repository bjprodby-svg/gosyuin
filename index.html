Know what to do before you step through the gate.

Seven illustrated guides explain the customs one step at a time, so a first visit does not feel like guesswork.

  • How to pray at a shrine, and how a temple differs
  • How to rinse your hands at the water basin
  • How to draw and read a fortune slip
  • Dress, photography and other manners

269 well-known shrines and temples, each with a written guide.

Every place on the map tells you what makes it special, what not to miss, when to go, how to get there and when it is open. Pick one, tap Go, and the app gives you a walking route with your progress on the Lock Screen, so the phone can stay in your pocket.

From Meiji Jingu and Senso-ji in Tokyo to Fushimi Inari Taisha and Kiyomizu-dera in Kyoto, Todai-ji in Nara and Itsukushima Jinja in Hiroshima.

When you are there, press a digital keepsake stamp. You have to be there.

Within about 100 meters of a shrine or temple on the map, the app invites you to press its stamp into your stamp book. Each of the 269 places has its own design, drawn in the style of a woodblock print. There is no way to press one from your sofa.

Your stamp book keeps every visit, shelved by prefecture, and nine yokai from Japanese folk tales join you one by one as it fills, from a paper lantern at your first place to the nine-tailed fox at the very end.

The stamps in this app are not goshuin.

They are original digital mementos, made for the app to mark that you were there. They are not copies of any shrine's or temple's seal.

A goshuin is a record of worship that you receive in person at the shrine or temple office, usually after paying your respects. Nothing on a phone replaces it. If you would like one, the Learn tab explains how to ask, and we would encourage you to.

Free, without an account

No sign-up, no ads

Open the app and use it. The app is free, with no ads and nothing locked. It is made independently, and an optional offering in the app helps keep it running.

Your stamp book stays on your iPhone

Your collection is stored on your device. We have no server and no accounts, so we never receive it.

Your location is not sent to us

It is used to show nearby places, guide your walk and confirm that you have arrived. Maps and routes come from Apple. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons, chosen in advance and credited to their photographers. The privacy policy has the details.

  • iPhone only. Requires iOS 26 or later. The interface is in English.
  • Background location, only when you ask. Walking guidance uses your location in the background only during a walk. If you turn on "Tell me when I'm near a shrine", iOS watches for nearby places on your iPhone and the app sends you one notification when you pass one. It's off until you turn it on.
  • Independent. GosyuinMap is an independent app and is not affiliated with any shrine or temple.
